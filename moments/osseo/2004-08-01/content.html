Exchange Year in Osseo

2004

Sixteen years old. Foster family. Osseo, Wisconsin — population small enough that the high school and the town are the same conversation. Osseo-Fairchild. American high school as a full-body transplant: Friday nights, lockers, a language I already spoke and a culture I didn't.

The hallway, not the signal

I had grown up on MTV, B.T.R., Friends, and Dallas — pirate dishes and a Bulgarian rock band and two American shows that felt like documents. Wisconsin was the moment that world stopped being a signal and became a hallway I had to walk down every morning. Foster family. Cafeteria. The particular silence of a small town after 8 p.m.

I landed in the year Friends actually ended. Fifty-two million people watched the finale. I had already watched those six people on a couch from Sofia. Now I was in the country the couch was in, and the show was over. That is a strange way to arrive.

Six months. That's the whole exchange: not a vacation, a transplant. It is the reason Chicago could happen next.
